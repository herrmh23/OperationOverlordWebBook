Before You Watch

Three Historical Film Sources

Part 5 asks you to examine authentic historical film as evidence. Select one of the three sources below, watch closely, and separate what you can directly observe from what you infer or learn from historical context.

Genre note: Film footage is an audio-visual primary source when it records people, places, actions, or events from the historical period being studied. Treat the camera as evidence—but remember that any filmed view captures only part of a larger event.
Viewing note: Wartime footage may include scenes of danger, injury, or medical treatment. Watch for historical evidence and use respectful, specific language in your observations.

Evidence Guide: Begin with what is visible or audible in the source. Add historical explanation only after you have recorded the evidence.

Good source analysis: “I observe…” and “This may suggest…” are not the same claim. Keep them separate.

Observation vs. Context

Historical Context: What Helps Explain the Film?

No single camera records the whole event. Keep source evidence separate from information supplied by historical context.

Source Evidence

What the film can show directly

People, objects, movement, landscapes, weather or visibility, vehicles, equipment, damage, and other details actually visible or audible in a filmed sequence.

Historical Context

What context helps explain

Names, dates, exact locations, military purposes, causes, outcomes, and larger significance—information that may not be visible in the footage and should not be guessed from appearance alone.
